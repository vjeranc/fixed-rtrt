The composition convention is RT(F) = R(T(F)), matching scripts/primitive_mathlib.py.

Definitions

Read a forest as a left-to-right list of rooted trees. In [node(C)] ++ S, the first root has child forest C, and S is the list of top-level trees sitting to its right.

  • R is the ordinary mirror of the whole forest. It reverses the order of the top-level roots, and it also reverses the child order inside every root, at every depth. No node changes depth; only left and right are flipped recursively.
  • T is the child-sibling swap. Split the forest into its first tree node(C) and the remaining sibling forest S. After T, the transformed old sibling forest T(S) becomes the child forest of the new first root, and the transformed old child forest T(C) becomes the remaining top-level forest. Then the same rule continues recursively.
  • Equivalently, make one new top-level root whose children are T(S). Then put the forest T(C) to the right of that root. Thus old siblings move down under the first root, and old children move up to become siblings to the right.
  • T does not preserve the number of top-level roots. Old siblings can move down and become children of the new first root, while old children of the first root can move up and become top-level siblings. So a forest with three top-level roots may become a forest with two, or one, or more, depending on the first tree.
  • RT means do T first and then mirror with R. The column (RT)^2 means apply that same RT move twice.
  • Example to keep in mind: T turns a vertical two-node chain into two side-by-side leaves, and T turns those two side-by-side leaves back into the vertical chain. R would only mirror left and right.

Other Coordinates

In Lukasiewicz coordinates, record every node by its number of children in preorder. A leaf is 0. A path version is obtained by replacing each outdegree k by the step k - 1. 

Forest F = [o(o o)  o(o)  o]
L(F)     = [2, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0]

To apply T, split a nonempty degree list into the first root, its child forest A, and the remaining sibling forest B: 

seq = [k] ++ A ++ B

A encodes exactly k child trees of the first root.
B encodes the top-level siblings to the right.

T_L([])              = []
T_L([k] ++ A ++ B)   = [roots(T_L(B))] ++ T_L(B) ++ T_L(A)

So T is still recursive in Lukasiewicz form. It is cleaner than the drawing, but not trivial.

The coordinate where T is trivial is the first-child/next-sibling binary tree:

In Binary(left,right), the left branch points to the first child of the forest root, and the right branch points to the next top-level sibling. 

Forest chain:        [A(B)]      binary pointer: A -first child-> B
Forest siblings:     [A  B]      binary pointer: A -next sibling-> B

T_b swaps these two pointer kinds everywhere, so a chain becomes siblings
and siblings become a chain.
In the n=6 example:
F = [A(B C)  D(E)  F]

A's first child is B, so the binary picture has A -first child-> B.
C is not a child of B in the forest. C is B's next sibling, so the binary
picture has B -next sibling-> C. This is drawn as B's right pointer.
Phi([])                  = empty
Phi([node(C)] ++ S)      = Binary(Phi(C), Phi(S))

T_binary(empty)          = empty
T_binary(Binary(L, R))    = Binary(T_binary(R), T_binary(L))

R_binary(empty)          = empty
R_binary(Binary(L, R))    = cat(R_binary(R), Binary(R_binary(L), empty))

In this binary representation, T is just recursive left-right mirror. The cost is that R becomes the less trivial operation: mirror the right side, then graft the mirrored left side onto the right spine.
